For grown-ups

Pip's Puzzle Island practises the thinking that comes before numbers: noticing, comparing, sorting, ordering and planning. Pip reads every instruction aloud, so no reading is needed. There is no timer and no losing: a wrong answer gets a friendly nudge, and after two tries the right answer glows.

Each game gets a little harder only when your child is ready, and eases off again if a level is too tricky. Every finished round earns a star, and stars grow the island with surprises.

  • Pattern TrainPatterns · prediction
    Finish the pattern on the train: AB, AAB, ABC and more, by colour, shape and size. Each item plays a note, so patterns can be heard as well as seen.
  • Hungry MonstersSorting · classification
    Feed each monster the cookies it likes: sort by colour, then shape, then size, then by two rules at once (red and square).
  • Bunny HopPlanning · early coding
    Plan Bunny's route with arrow cards, press Go, then spot and fix mistakes. This is sequencing and debugging, the core ideas of programming.
  • Odd One OutComparing · reasoning
    Find the one that is different. Later rounds hide the clue among other differences, so your child has to work out which feature matters.
  • Tower BuilderOrdering · seriation
    Stack rings from biggest to smallest and build stairs from shortest to tallest, which is logical ordering without counting.

Play together

  • After a success, ask “How did you know?” Explaining a reason is where the thinking deepens.
  • Let mistakes happen. In Bunny Hop, finding what went wrong is the lesson.
  • Short sessions of 10–15 minutes work best at this age.
